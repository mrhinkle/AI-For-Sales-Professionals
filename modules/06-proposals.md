# Proposals and the paperwork

The words around the numbers get written for you. The numbers are still yours.

notes:
Proposals and RFPs are where sellers lose evenings. This is the module that gives them back, with one hard rule about numbers.

## First draft from your template and the call

```prompt
Using our proposal template [paste], my product file, and the transcript and notes from [Company] [paste],
draft the proposal. Restate their problem in their own words from the transcript. Map each need to
what {{product}} does, with the proof we can name. Leave every price, discount, and date as [TBD]
for me to fill in. Add a mutual action plan with the steps between today and signature.
Flag any promise in the draft that isn't in my product file.
```

notes:
Two protections are built in. Prices and dates stay as TBD so the model never invents a number that ends up in front of a buyer. And the last line catches the thing proposals are famous for: a promise that sales made and the product doesn't keep. Read every flag.

## The same move works for RFPs and the business case

- RFPs: keep your past answers in a file. It drafts new answers from old ones and marks the gaps
- Business case: give it their numbers from the call and ask for the math shown step by step
- Executive summary: ask for one paragraph a CFO would read, then one a CTO would read
- Every number gets checked by a human before it leaves the building. No exceptions

notes:
The RFP library is a real asset once you have it. Twenty answered RFPs in a folder and the next one is a day's work instead of a week's. The business case: insist on shown math. If it can't show the arithmetic, it made the number up.
