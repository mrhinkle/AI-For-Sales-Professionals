# The rules that keep you employed

Four of them. None are complicated.

notes:
Short module, serious tone. These aren't legal boilerplate. Every one of them is a story I've watched happen to someone.

## Four rules

### 01 | Some things never go in
Customer confidential data, personal data beyond a name and title, unreleased pricing or roadmap. Unless your company's tool is approved for it, it stays out.

### 02 | Use the company seat
The paid account has the data retention and training settings your security team agreed to. Your personal login doesn't.

### 03 | Check before you trust
Names, numbers, dates, quotes. If you'd be embarrassed to be wrong about it, click the source.

### 04 | It never sends
Email law, your brand, your relationship. The assistant drafts. A person reads and sends.

notes:
Rule one is the one people break by accident, usually by pasting a whole transcript with a customer's architecture in it into a free consumer tool. Ask your security team which tool is approved and what can go in it. Rule two is the fix for most of rule one. Rules three and four you've heard all session.

# At 99 percent accuracy, a thousand automated sends is ten mistakes with *your signature* on them.

Errors compound at machine speed. The human gate is the circuit breaker.

notes:
This is why the one rule exists. A model that's right 99 times out of 100 feels perfect in a demo. Run it unattended across a thousand contacts and the ten bad ones are the ones that get forwarded. Keep the person in the loop where the name goes on it.
