# About half of a seller's week isn't selling.

It's reading, looking things up, writing the same email a slightly different way, and typing into {{crm}}. That half is what we're going after today.

notes:
Ask for a show of hands: who spent more than an hour yesterday on research, drafting, or CRM updates? Everybody. That's the target. We are not replacing the part where you build trust and read a room. We're buying that part more hours.

## What it's good at today, and what it isn't

### Good at | Reading and summarizing
A 10-K, forty job postings, a two-hour transcript. Faster than you, and it doesn't skim.

### Good at | First drafts and rehearsal
Email, follow-ups, proposals, and playing the skeptical buyer so you can practice.

### Not yet | Knowing your buyer
It has never met them. It doesn't know what was said at the last QBR unless you tell it.

### Not yet | Judgment and relationships
When to push, when to walk, who actually signs. That's still you, and it's the paid part.

notes:
Keep this honest. The tools are good at volume and language. They are bad at truth they haven't been given and at judgment. Think of a very well-read new hire on their first day: eager, fast, and knows nothing about your accounts until you tell them. Everything we do today is about telling them.

## Your week, with an assistant in the chair next to you

### Research | Account brief before the first call
One prompt, a company name, ten minutes. Sources attached.

### Prospect | Who to call, and why now
Rank a list against your ICP file and surface the trigger events.

### Reach out | First touch that sounds like you
Under ninety words, one ask, written in your voice from your examples.

### Meet | Prep sheet, then listen
Five questions and the likely objections. Recorder on so you can stop typing.

### Follow up | Summary and next steps in ten minutes
From the transcript: email, CRM notes, tasks.

### Propose | First draft from your template and the call
You check the numbers. It writes the words around them.

notes:
This is the map for the rest of the session. Every row is a module. The pattern is the same in every one: you give it the source material, it drafts, you check and send. Point, don't read. We'll do the top rows hands-on.

## You probably already own the tools

- A general assistant: {{assistant}}. One is plenty. Use the paid company seat
- A meeting recorder: {{recorder}}, or the one built into your video calls
- Your CRM's assistant: {{crm}} ships one now. It's fine for notes and summaries
- Enrichment and sequencing: {{sequencer}} for the lists and sends. Keep the AI in the drafting seat

notes:
Resist the urge to buy anything this quarter. The general assistant plus a recorder gets you ninety percent of what we'll cover. The CRM copilots are good at the boring parts, which is exactly where you want them. Tool shopping is procrastination with a budget.
